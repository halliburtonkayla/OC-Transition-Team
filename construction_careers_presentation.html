Carpenter • laborer • equipment operator • concrete worker • roofer • estimator • supervisor and many skilled trades.

Reading the Job

Construction work follows plans, measurements, specifications, schedules, and instructions from supervisors and skilled tradespeople.

4 / 15

Measurement Skills

Tape measures, fractions, angles, levels, area, quantities, and careful marking are everyday skills in many trades.

5 / 15
🛠️

Tools

Hammers • drills • saws • levels • measuring tools • fasteners • specialty tools depending on the trade.

6 / 15
⛑️

Safety First

PPE, fall protection when required, equipment rules, hazard communication, housekeeping, and never using equipment without proper training.

7 / 15
🤝

Teamwork

A job site depends on people communicating clearly because one crew's work can affect another crew's schedule and safety.

8 / 15
🎓

Training Paths

Career/technical education, apprenticeships, employer training, trade schools, and community colleges can provide pathways into construction occupations.

9 / 15
💬

Workplace Scenario

You notice a hazard in the work area. Stop exposing yourself to the hazard, warn others as appropriate, and report it according to site procedures.

10 / 15
⭐

Quality Work

Measure carefully, follow plans, use the correct materials, inspect work, and fix problems before they become bigger problems.

11 / 15
🌦️

Weather & Conditions

Some construction jobs involve outdoor weather, noise, dust, heights, physical work, early schedules, or travel between job sites.

12 / 15
📈

Moving Up

Workers may gain trade skills, complete apprenticeships/certifications, become journey-level workers, crew leaders, supervisors, estimators, or contractors where requirements are met.
