🍦 Our Ice Cream Shop
Primary Pre-ETS: Workplace Readiness TrainingTime: 30–60 minutesGroup: 3–5 students

Goal

Students practice teamwork, communication, following a work process, customer service, basic money decisions, and completing an assigned job role in a simulated ice cream shop.

Staff Instructions

Materials

  • Printed student worksheet for each team
  • Pencils, calculators, scrap paper
  • Optional: cups/cones, spoons, paper toppings, play money, order slips
  • Optional real-food version only if your school allows it and allergy/sanitation procedures are followed

Set Up

  1. Put students in teams of 3–5.
  2. Tell them they are opening a small ice cream shop for one class period.
  3. Give the team a starting supply budget of $25.00.
  4. Have students choose jobs before they begin planning.

Suggested Roles

ManagerKeeps the team on task and checks the final plan.
Cashier / Order TakerGreets the customer, records the order, and totals the price.
Prep WorkerBuilds the mock order exactly as written.
ServerChecks the order and delivers it to the correct customer.
Inventory HelperTracks supplies and watches the budget.

Activity Steps

  1. Students name the shop and choose 2 ice cream flavors and 4 toppings.
  2. They use the worksheet supply list to stay under the $25 budget.
  3. They set menu prices that are higher than their approximate cost.
  4. Each student completes their assigned job.
  5. Use 3–5 sample customers. Customers give a name and place an order.
  6. The cashier writes the order; prep worker makes it; server must remember which customer receives it.
  7. After the round, students complete the reflection section together.
30-minute version: Skip the full budget math. Give the team a ready-made supply list, have them set prices, assign jobs, and complete three customer orders.

Staff Look-Fors

  • Does the student stay in the assigned role?
  • Does the student communicate respectfully with teammates?
  • Can the student follow the order from beginning to end?
  • Can the student ask for clarification instead of guessing?
  • Can the team correct an order mistake appropriately?

Quick Debrief Questions

  1. Which job felt easiest? Why?
  2. Which job required the most communication?
  3. What happened when the shop got busy?
  4. What would the team change before opening again?

💡 Build a Business
Primary Pre-ETS: Job Exploration CounselingTime: 30–60 minutesGroup: 2–4 students

Goal

Students explore different job functions inside a business by identifying a customer need, choosing a product or service, assigning roles, and explaining how each role contributes to the business.

Staff Instructions

  1. Divide students into teams of 2–4.
  2. Each team chooses a problem customers have and creates a business that solves it.
  3. Students name the business and choose at least four jobs the business needs.
  4. Students complete the guided worksheet and prepare a 60-second pitch.
  5. After presentations, ask students which job they would personally choose and why.

Examples

  • Mobile car wash
  • Pet-sitting service
  • Snack stand
  • Lawn-care company
  • Photography business
  • Childcare helper service

Staff Look-Fors

  • Can the student connect job duties to a real business need?
  • Can the student identify skills needed for a job?
  • Can the student explain a job they might or might not enjoy?

🍽️ Restaurant Team Challenge
Primary Pre-ETS: Workplace Readiness TrainingTime: 30–60 minutesGroup: 4–6 students

Goal

Students practice food-service workplace roles, taking accurate orders, teamwork, sequencing, customer communication, and remembering which finished order belongs to each customer.

Staff Instructions

Roles

  • Host / Greeter
  • Server / Order Taker
  • Prep Cook
  • Line Cook / Assembler
  • Cashier
  • Manager / Quality Check

Steps

  1. Give each team a simple 5-item menu.
  2. Assign or let students choose roles.
  3. Seat 3–5 pretend customers at numbered tables.
  4. Server takes each order and writes the table number.
  5. Kitchen team prepares a paper/mock version of the meal.
  6. Manager checks the order before service.
  7. Server delivers each meal to the correct table.
  8. Cashier calculates each check.
Use paper food cards if real food is not appropriate. The key skill is the workflow, not actual cooking.

💼 Mock Job Fair
Primary Pre-ETS: Workplace Readiness TrainingTime: 30–50 minutesGroup: 4+ students

Goal

Students practice professional introductions, asking job-related questions, listening for job requirements, and comparing workplace expectations.

Staff Instructions

  1. Choose 3–5 employers: grocery store, daycare, hospital, restaurant, auto shop, hotel, warehouse, etc.
  2. Assign some students as employer representatives and some as applicants.
  3. Give each employer a job card with duties and requirements.
  4. Applicants rotate every 4–5 minutes and use the worksheet to ask questions.
  5. Halfway through, switch roles so everyone practices both sides.

Applicant Must Practice

  • Greeting and eye contact / appropriate alternative
  • “Tell me about the job.”
  • “What skills do you look for?”
  • “What hours would I work?”
  • Thank-you / closing statement

Employer Cards

Grocery Stocker: lift items, organize shelves, follow directions, evening/weekend availability.

Childcare Assistant: supervise children, clean, communicate, be dependable.

Restaurant Crew: take orders, prep food, clean, work quickly with a team.

Auto Shop Helper: organize tools, clean work area, assist technician, follow safety rules.

🎤 Interview Team Practice
Primary Pre-ETS: Workplace Readiness TrainingTime: 25–45 minutesGroup: 3 per team

Goal

Students practice answering career-specific interview questions, listening, taking turns, and giving respectful feedback.

Staff Instructions

  1. Put students in groups of three: applicant, interviewer, feedback coach.
  2. Applicant chooses a job they would actually consider.
  3. Interviewer uses the six guided questions.
  4. Feedback coach marks strengths and one improvement.
  5. Rotate roles until every student has interviewed.

Career-Specific Prompt

After the general questions, the interviewer should add one question about the student's chosen job. Example: “A customer is upset at the register. What would you do?”

Six Core Questions

  1. Tell me about yourself.
  2. Why do you want this job?
  3. What is one strength you would bring?
  4. Tell me about a time you worked with others.
  5. What would you do if you did not understand a task?
  6. Why should we hire you?

💵 First Paycheck Budget Team
Primary Pre-ETS: Workplace Readiness TrainingTime: 30–45 minutesGroup: 2–4 students

Goal

Students practice planning with limited income, prioritizing needs, discussing trade-offs, and making a group decision.

Staff Instructions

  1. Tell the team they have $1,800 take-home pay for the month.
  2. They must pay every required bill first.
  3. They then choose optional spending while keeping at least $100 for emergencies.
  4. Require the group to explain every optional choice.
  5. At the end, give them one surprise card: tire repair $175, missed work loses $90, or bonus shift earns $120.

🎓 Plan My Next Step
Primary Pre-ETS: Counseling on Postsecondary Education OpportunitiesTime: 30–50 minutesGroup: 2–4 students

Goal

Students compare education and training routes after high school and identify questions they need answered before choosing a path.

Staff Instructions

  1. Assign each team four paths: 4-year college, community college, TCAT/trade program, and direct-to-work with employer training.
  2. Students compare length, cost, admissions, transportation, and job outcome.
  3. Each student chooses one path they want to explore further.
  4. End with one action step for the student to complete later.

Keep the Focus

This activity is not about picking a “best” path. It is about understanding the differences and matching a path to the student's career goal and support needs.

🗣️ Speak Up at Work
Primary Pre-ETS: Instruction in Self-AdvocacyTime: 25–40 minutesGroup: 3–5 students

Goal

Students practice identifying a workplace need, choosing the right person to approach, and using clear respectful language to ask for help, clarification, or a reasonable change.

Staff Instructions

  1. Give each group one scenario.
  2. Students identify the problem, the person they should talk to, and what they should say.
  3. Assign roles: employee, supervisor, coworker, observer.
  4. Students role-play the conversation.
  5. Observer gives one strength and one suggestion.

Scenario Choices

  • Your supervisor gives you two tasks and you do not know which is first.
  • You cannot hear instructions because the work area is too loud.
  • Your ride will make you 10 minutes late.
  • A coworker keeps asking you to do their tasks.
  • You do not understand how to use a piece of equipment.

Self-Advocacy Formula

1. State the situation.
“I want to make sure I do this correctly.”

2. Say what you need.
“Can you show me the first step again?”

3. Confirm the plan.
“Okay, I will start with this task and then do the other one.”
