Electrician Careers

Explore a skilled trade that powers homes, businesses, industry, and infrastructure.

2 / 15
🔌

What Electricians Do

Install, maintain, inspect, troubleshoot, and repair electrical systems according to plans, codes, training, and safety procedures.

3 / 15
🏢

Where They Work

Homes • commercial buildings • construction sites • factories • maintenance departments • infrastructure projects.

4 / 15
💡

Types of Work

Residential • commercial • industrial • maintenance • controls and specialty electrical work.

5 / 15
📐

Plans & Measurements

Electricians use diagrams, plans, measurements, calculations, labels, and specifications to complete work accurately.

6 / 15
🦺

Safety Is Essential

Electrical hazards can be deadly. Qualified training, de-energizing procedures, verification, PPE, codes, and workplace safety rules are critical.

7 / 15
🎓

Training Path

Apprenticeship is a common pathway combining paid work experience and instruction. Licensing/certification requirements vary by location and type of work.

8 / 15
🧰

Tools

Meters/test equipment • hand tools • conduit tools • ladders/lifts where trained • labeling and measuring tools.

9 / 15
🧠

Troubleshooting

Good troubleshooting follows a process: understand the problem, gather information, test safely, identify the cause, repair correctly, and verify.

10 / 15
💬

Workplace Scenario

A circuit or piece of equipment appears unsafe. Do not improvise or work beyond your training—secure/report the situation and follow established electrical-safety procedures.

11 / 15
⭐

Professional Skills

Dependability • precision • math • reading diagrams • communication • documentation • respect for safety rules.

12 / 15
📈

Moving Up

Apprentice → experienced/journey-level worker where applicable → specialty/leadership roles, foreperson, estimator, inspector, or contractor subject to requirements.

13 / 15
🤔

Is This Career for You?

Do you like hands-on problem-solving, careful measurements, technical learning, and following detailed safety procedures?

Try It Safely

Use a paper diagram or classroom circuit simulator—not household wiring—to identify symbols, trace a circuit, and explain the path electricity would take.
