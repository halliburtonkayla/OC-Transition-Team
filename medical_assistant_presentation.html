Medical Assistant Careers

Medical assistants help healthcare offices run smoothly and support patients and clinical teams.

2/15
🎯

Two Sides of the Job

Medical assisting can include administrative tasks and clinical tasks, depending on training, law, employer, and role.

3/15
⭐

Administrative Tasks

Scheduling, phones, records, forms, patient check-in, messages, and other office responsibilities.

4/15
📚

Clinical Support

Within permitted scope: preparing rooms, measuring vital signs, documenting information, assisting clinicians, and other trained duties.

5/15
💡

Where They Work

Physician offices, clinics, outpatient centers, specialty practices, and other ambulatory healthcare settings.

6/15
🛠️

Patient Communication

Greet patients respectfully, listen, protect privacy, explain your role, and know when a concern needs a nurse or clinician.

7/15
🎯

Infection Control

Hand hygiene, PPE, sharps safety where applicable, cleaning, and following workplace infection-control procedures.

8/15
⭐

Training

Medical assisting programs vary. Employers may prefer or require education, certification, or specific competencies depending on the position.

9/15
📚

Technology

Electronic health records, scheduling systems, phones, patient portals, vital-sign equipment, and office technology.

10/15
💡

Workplace Scenario

A patient asks you to diagnose their symptoms. Do not work outside your role; document/relay the concern to the appropriate licensed professional.

11/15
🛠️

Professional Skills

Confidentiality, accuracy, compassion, multitasking, communication, teamwork, and dependable attendance.

12/15
🎯

Busy Clinic

Prioritize safely, communicate delays, document accurately, and ask for help when several tasks compete for attention.

13/15
⭐

Moving Up

Experience can support further education in nursing, health administration, clinical specialties, or other healthcare careers.

14/15
📚

Career Check

Which tasks are administrative? Which are clinical support? Why is knowing your scope important?

15/15
💡

Try It

Practice a mock patient check-in: greeting, confirming information appropriately, protecting privacy, taking a message, and directing the patient to the next step.
