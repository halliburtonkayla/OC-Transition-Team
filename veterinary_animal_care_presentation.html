Veterinary & Animal Care

Loving animals is a start—animal careers also require responsibility, science, cleaning, communication, and teamwork.

2 / 15
🐶

Career Options

Veterinarian • veterinary technician/technologist • veterinary assistant • kennel attendant • groomer • animal-care worker.

3 / 15
🐕

Daily Animal Care

Feeding, cleaning, observation, safe handling, exercise, documentation, and communicating concerns may be part of animal-care roles.

4 / 15
🩺

Veterinary Team

Veterinarians diagnose and treat within their professional scope; credentialed technicians and assistants support care according to their training, law, and workplace procedures.

5 / 15
🧼

Cleaning Matters

Animal-care workplaces require frequent cleaning and infection-control practices. Caring about animals includes doing the less glamorous work.

6 / 15
🦮

Safe Handling

Animals may be scared, sick, painful, or unpredictable. Staff use approved handling/restraint techniques and ask trained coworkers for help.

7 / 15
🎓

Education Paths

Education and credential requirements differ greatly by role—from on-the-job animal-care positions to formal veterinary technology programs and professional veterinary degrees.

8 / 15
⚕️

Tools & Technology

Scales • records systems • animal-care equipment • diagnostic/treatment equipment used by appropriately trained staff.

9 / 15
🗣️

People Skills Too

Animal professionals communicate with owners, explain instructions, listen to concerns, handle emotional situations, and work with a team.

10 / 15
💬

Workplace Scenario

A normally friendly animal becomes fearful and growls. Do not force the situation; follow handling procedures and get appropriate trained assistance.

11 / 15
⭐

Professional Skills

Observation • patience • reliability • sanitation • empathy • documentation • teamwork.

12 / 15
📈

Moving Up

Animal-care experience can help students explore grooming, veterinary assisting, veterinary technology, animal science, management, or further professional education.

13 / 15
🤔

Can you handle cleaning, smells, nervous animals, emotional owners, and detailed instructions—not just playing with pets?
